Since apartheid's end in 1994, South Africa has made significant strides in economic and social development, yet substantial disparities persist. GDP per capita tripled by 2015, poverty rates fell from 66% to 53% by 2011, and life expectancy rose from 53 to 62 years. Black middle class grew from 1.6 million in 1993 to 10.5 million in 2015. However, inequality remains high, with a Gini coefficient of 0.63. Apartheid's legacy of racial disparities in wealth, education, and opportunity persists. Political structures have democratized, with the African National Congress (ANC) dominant since 1994. Social cohesion has improved but remains fragile, with racial tensions and xenophobia incidents. Reforms include Black Economic Empowerment (BEE) policies, land redistribution, and improved access to education and healthcare. Further steps include accelerating land reform, improving education quality, enhancing labor market policies, and fostering inclusive economic growth.
