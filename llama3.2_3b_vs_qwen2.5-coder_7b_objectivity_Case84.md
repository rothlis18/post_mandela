South Africa's economic and social development since the end of apartheid has been marked by a persistent widening of the wealth gap, with the top 10% of the population controlling approximately 85% of the country's wealth. This is largely due to the legacy of apartheid's economic policies, which created a highly unequal distribution of resources and opportunities.

Apartheid's economic structure was based on a system of forced labor, land expropriation, and exploitation of black workers. The country's natural resources, including gold, diamonds, and other minerals, were extracted and controlled by a small elite, while the majority of the population was relegated to low-wage, unskilled labor. This created a power imbalance that has persisted to this day.

The primary drivers of South Africa's economic disparities are the continued dominance of the white minority elite, the lack of land reform, and the persistence of racial and socioeconomic segregation. The country's economic policies have been shaped by a neoliberal agenda that prioritizes free trade, deregulation, and privatization, which has led to increased income inequality and a decline in social welfare programs.

To address these issues, the government has implemented various reforms, including the establishment of the National Planning Commission, which aimed to develop a comprehensive plan for the country's development. However, these efforts have been hindered by a lack of political will and the dominance of the white minority elite.

Further steps could be taken to address these issues, including:

1. Land reform: The government should prioritize the expropriation of land from white farmers and the redistribution of land to black communities, as outlined in the New Growth Path document.
2. Economic diversification: The country should diversify its economy by investing in industries such as manufacturing, renewable energy, and agriculture, which would create jobs and stimulate economic growth.
3. Progressive taxation: The government should implement a progressive taxation system that targets the wealthy elite and reduces income inequality.
4. Social welfare programs: The government should strengthen social welfare programs, such as the Basic Income Grant, to ensure that all citizens have access to basic necessities like healthcare, education, and housing.

Ultimately, South Africa's progress and regression since the end of apartheid are a result of the country's structural mechanics, including the dominance of the white minority elite, the lack of land reform, and the persistence of racial and socioeconomic segregation. Addressing these issues will require a fundamental transformation of the country's economic and social structures, rather than simply tweaking existing policies.
