South Africa's economic and social development since the end of apartheid can be analyzed through the following facts:

1. GDP per capita has increased from approximately $3,400 in 1994 to around $6,400 in 2020, a 88% increase.
2. The country's poverty rate decreased from 34.4% in 1993 to 27.5% in 2020.
3. The unemployment rate has fluctuated between 25% and 30% since 1994, with a peak of 28.5% in 2019.
4. The country's economic growth has been driven primarily by the mining and agriculture sectors.
5. The apartheid legacy has contributed to ongoing economic disparities, with the top 10% of the population holding approximately 85% of the country's wealth.

Primary drivers of these changes include:

1. The implementation of the Growth, Employment and Redistribution (GEAR) policy in 1997, which aimed to promote economic growth and reduce poverty.
2. The introduction of the Black Economic Empowerment (BEE) policy in 2003, which aimed to increase black ownership and control in the economy.
3. The National Development Plan (NDP) 2030, which aims to achieve a middle-income economy by 2030 and reduce poverty and inequality.

Systemic reforms implemented to address these issues include:

1. The establishment of the National Planning Commission (NPC) to develop and implement the NDP.
2. The creation of the South African Revenue Service (SARS) to improve tax collection and reduce tax evasion.
3. The introduction of the Basic Income Grant (BIG) pilot project in 2019, which aims to provide a guaranteed minimum income to the poor.

Further steps that could be taken to ensure sustained progress and equality for all citizens include:

1. Implementing a more progressive tax system to reduce income inequality.
2. Increasing investment in education and skills training to improve employability.
3. Implementing policies to promote greater black economic empowerment and ownership.
