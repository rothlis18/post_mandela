Since the end of apartheid in 1994, South Africa has experienced significant economic and social development, yet substantial disparities persist. Economic growth has been tepid, with a high unemployment rate and income inequality remaining stubbornly high. Political structures have transitioned from apartheid's authoritarianism to a democratic framework, but corruption and patronage continue to undermine governance. Social cohesion is strained by racial tensions and economic grievances. Systemic reforms include land redistribution, affirmative action policies, and anti-corruption initiatives, but progress is slow. Further steps include implementing fiscal discipline, promoting inclusive economic policies, and addressing systemic racism in education and employment.
